Water pan siting — V02 pipeline report

KAZA TFCA, Zambezi Region, Namibia — methodology summary, generated as a standalone reference alongside the interactive explorer.

Objective

Identify and rank candidate sites for artificial water pans, balancing biophysical suitability, wildlife needs, cost, and human-wildlife conflict risk. Rather than a single weighting, 7 scenarios are scored in parallel — six that each emphasize one category, plus one balanced scenario — so a decision-maker can see how the ranking shifts depending on what's prioritized.

Pipeline

1

Data collection

~29 raw variables pulled from Earth Engine, CHELSA, HiHydroSoil, OpenStreetMap, TUM Global Building Atlas, and Naidoo et al. 2024 elephant GPS data — see the variable table below.

2

Harmonization

Every layer reprojected to EPSG:32734 and resampled onto one shared 100m grid — average downsample for finer sources, bilinear upsample for coarser ones.

3

Normalization

Each criterion percentile-clipped (2nd–98th) and rescaled 0–1, direction-corrected so higher always means more suitable. Sparse/mostly-zero layers fall back to true min/max to avoid a divide-by-zero collapse.

4

Category sub-indices

Criteria combine into 6 category scores via a weighted average (weights sum to 1.0 per category, enforced by an assertion).

5

Scenario scoring

6 category sub-indices combine into 7 scenario suitability scores — computed identically at both the 100m raster level and the 1km² hexagon level (see note below on why these differ slightly).

Scenario system

Rather than hand-picked weights, scenarios are generated by a single rule: the emphasized category gets 0.6, the other five split the remaining 0.4 equally (0.08 each). The balanced scenario weights all six equally (1/6).

Raster vs. hex outputs differ slightly, by design. Both are computed with identical formulas and weights, but percentile normalization is calculated against a different underlying distribution in each case — the full population of 100m pixels for the raster version, versus the population of hexagon means for the hex version. Neither is "more correct"; the hex version is the primary decision-making output, the raster version is a finer-grained sanity check on whether hex-averaging smoothed over anything important.

Key methodological decisions

DecisionReasoning
CHELSA over WorldClim1981–2010 baseline overlaps better in time with the rest of the pipeline's recent-year data than WorldClim's 1960–1991
MCD64A1 over Sentinel-2 NBR for fireThe original NBR-threshold method misread ordinary dry-season leaf drop as fire across most of the landscape
RPI computed from a 9×9 smoothed DEM, clamped ±1Raw differentiation amplified SRTM sensor noise; validated afterward against JRC surface water (necessary-but-not-sufficient pattern confirmed)
Dist. ESW replaces 4 recurrence bandsDistance answers the actual siting question (connectivity to existing ephemeral water); a stepping-stone network for small, poorly-mobile fauna
Dist. permanent water: farther = betterMatches the project's core goal — bring water to areas where it isn't already, not site redundantly near existing sources
Micro-corridors droppedNaturally sparse (>2% of AOI exactly zero), causing percentile normalization to degenerate; unlikely to discriminate meaningfully between most hexagons here regardless

Known gaps

Not automatable, still requiring direct requests: conservancy zoning (IRDNC/NACSO), human-wildlife conflict incident records (MET Namibia/IRDNC), customary land use/grazing areas. NDVI's MODIS scale factor (×0.0001) needs re-verifying against the current file before treating its raw values as literal NDVI.
